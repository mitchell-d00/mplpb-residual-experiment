Denominators and scoring
Ineligible serving: retrieval returned an explicitly labelled ineligible ID,
among 11 fixtures containing a query-relevant ineligible page. This is a
per-query opportunity rate, not a rate per served page. Ambiguity: returned
kind=ambiguous on the one fixture with two independent eligible scope owners.
Stale rejection: no source returned on either stale-derivative fixture.
False promotion: answer_supported=true among eight fixtures without a unique
eligible, supported answer. This includes ambiguity and eligibility failures,
not only the single body-support-negative control. Coverage: a supported answer
from a labelled eligible, supporting ID on the six answerable queries. The
first and revised same-owner cases are correlated controls, not independent
statistical observations. No confidence interval or population estimate is
implied by these small, purpose-built counts.

Interpretation
C and D match on every normalized read and answer decision in this suite.
The independent conventional gate therefore matches the observed stop/refuse
behavior on the declared subset. That limits a claim of architectural necessity
or superiority. A/B merely expose what happens when these particular consumers
omit eligibility and support checks; they do not stand for every conventional
system or prove that Git cannot enforce such checks.

The body-support control is decisive for the paper's retrieval distinction:
in 'unsupported', C and D retrieve the valid DOC-0002, but both strict answer
paths refuse. A/B are intentionally constructed consumers that promote source
text. Their promotion errors are not attributed to the bare storage format.

The damaged-successor fixture explicitly restores the predecessor's declared
status to current to model an incomplete revision (the published P2 setup).
Damaging a successor does not itself undo an already recorded retirement or
withdrawal. This setup is retained in the runner and fixture bytes.
